# Disclosure rules

Research baseline: 2026-09-10. Prototype guidance, not a legal opinion. Before using it for a new deployment, check the official sources below for changes. Preserve the dated basis of decisions. Do not invent an update if access is unavailable; record that limitation.

## Scope

Article 50 transparency is not all website law. Determine EU applicability and whether the customer is a provider, deployer/publisher or both. Copyright, privacy, consumer protection, prohibited AI uses and high-risk duties require separate assessment. Emotion recognition and biometric categorisation also have duties outside this prototype.

AI-written source code alone does not require a public site label. A publisher's visible disclosure is separate from a generative system provider's machine-readable marking and detectability duties. Neither a custom HTML attribute nor a visible badge proves the latter. Do not remove existing provenance. Treat missing credentials as unknown.

## Publisher content

- AI-generated/manipulated public-interest text: disclose unless a supported exception applies. Substantive human review or editorial control AND editorial responsibility can establish the text exception. Automated review, proofreading or a policy alone cannot. A substantive AI edit after review invalidates it. This exception does not cover media.
- Deepfake image/audio/video: disclose. Assess resemblance and potentially misleading authenticity in context; the test extends beyond impersonating named people. Ordinary marketing is not a universal media exemption.
- Evidently creative, artistic, satirical or fictional deepfakes: tailored, less intrusive disclosure, not automatic exemption. Apply case-specific conditions.
- Ordinary text outside the public-interest publication test may not require a label. Health, safety, sustainability or investor claims can change that assessment.
- Unknown origin, applicability or context: resolve facts; do not silently call it human or label it AI as a guess.

## Timing and placement

The general applicability date is 2026-08-02. A limited provider marking transition to 2026-12-02 does not defer publisher labels or chatbot notices. Guidelines paragraph 154 distinguishes pre-date media from text generated/manipulated AND published before the date. Older text first published later can require disclosure. Reassess new modifications; do not infer exemption solely from file age.

Use the Code's icon/label approach: essential information must be clear and perceivable by first exposure, without clicking. Text can use a headline/start label. Standard deepfake media needs an embedded label or equivalent on-content presentation. Video/audio must account for late entry, interruptions and reuse; audio needs audible disclosure. Qualifying creative works can use adjacent contextual notices. Short-text contextual/session notices are a conditional accommodation, not blanket website coverage. Do not prescribe arbitrary legal font sizes or pixel minima.

Chatbots: provide notice at session start unless a supported obvious-interaction exception applies. User consent/acceptance is not generally required just to disclose AI. Avoid a consent-style popup as the default.

## Evidence and verification

Record origin evidence, generation/modification/publication dates, assessed context, current version and responsible publisher. Version-bound review is an engineering safeguard; the Code does not mandate logging every individual editorial review. Public editorial contact and appropriate internal procedures matter when relying on the exception. Limit personal data and confidential prompts.

Test rendered disclosures and supported exports. Manifest assessment is not a browser test, a complete inventory, a factual-origin detector or certification. An unsupported iframe/canvas/native surface remains a gap until integrated. Do not disable an existing site to conceal gaps.
